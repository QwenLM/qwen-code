# Managed agent teams: the lead-side runtime (H4e-b1)

[English](2026-10-10-managed-agent-team-lead-runtime.md) | [简体中文](2026-10-10-managed-agent-team-lead-runtime.zh-CN.md)

Status: implemented and enabled. #13824 landed the lead's team funnel, the five team tools and the Agent tool's `name` on the Hosted path, the `<teammate>` label, `task_list`'s pre-approval and the input previews, and the reopen whitelist, with both domains disabled. The enablement change adds `team_state` and `team_task` to the enabled list after the physical acceptance pass recorded under the Validation plan, together with the one rule that pass changed (decision 5, a finished member as the owner of a completed task). `team_message` and `team_plan` stay disabled. This is the first runtime slice of **H4e** of [#12827](https://github.com/QwenLM/qwen-code/issues/12827), stage H of the Managed Agent proposal [#12380](https://github.com/QwenLM/qwen-code/issues/12380), tracked by [#13745](https://github.com/QwenLM/qwen-code/issues/13745). It builds on H4b ([child Session runtime](2026-10-07-managed-child-session-runtime.md), #13550) and H4e-a ([team record contract](2026-10-10-managed-agent-teams.md), #13811), whose four team bodies and lead-Session rules it produces for the first time.

## Problem and scope

H4e-a pinned what a team may commit: a roster and lifecycle (`team_state`), a board (`team_task`), a mailbox (`team_message`) and plan approvals (`team_plan`), all in the lead Session's journal, all disabled. Nothing produces them. On the Hosted path a lead can launch child agents (H4b), but it cannot name them, group them, give them a board, or delete a team; `name` is refused at admission as a legacy-only argument, and no team tool is declared.

The H4e-a design gave the whole runtime to "H4e-b". That slice is too large to land in one piece, and part of it waits on work that has not started. The mailbox relay, the member-side delivery and the continuation of a member all build on H4d-b, the Session message runtime ([H4d-a design](2026-10-09-managed-session-messages.md)), which has no implementation yet; plan approval and member shutdown travel as mailbox messages. H4e-b is therefore split into three slices:

| Slice                    | Scope                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Waits on    |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- |
| **H4e-b1** (this design) | The lead-side team: `team_create` and `team_delete`, the managed Agent tool's `name` for spawning members, the lead's board (`task_create`, `task_update`, `task_list`), labeled member reports, and the close cascade under teams (#13745 E3). Enables `team_state` and `team_task`. | nothing new |
| H4e-b2                   | The mailbox: `team_message` relay, the team route of `send_message`, member-side delivery and receipts, member-side team tools (a member reading and updating the board through a command to the lead), assignment delivery, and continued members. Enables `team_message`.           | H4d-b       |
| H4e-b3                   | Plan approval (`team_plan`, `plan_mode_required`, `team_plan_approval` through D6 actions), `request_shutdown`, legacy team import, and the last enablement after a physical acceptance pass.                                                                                         | H4e-b2      |

H4e-b1 needs nothing that has not landed. Its members are H4b child agents, so creation, the relay, quotas, result delivery and the close cascade are H4b's, unchanged. What H4e-b1 adds is the team around them.

## Current state

The facts below are from `main` at `f733ffbd4a`.

- **Records.** The four team bodies are registered and validated in both languages, with the H4e-a rules: a team is led by its own Session; a member joins as a live child Session run that no other team lists; a task, message or plan request opens only in an active team; task numbers are unique per team; owners are `leader` or members; dependencies stay in the team and close no cycle. None of the four domains is in `MANAGED_SESSION_ENABLED_DOMAINS`. Java has no enablement list: its store validates every registered body, so the server-first order already holds for these domains.
- **The Hosted Agent tool.** `HOSTED_AGENT_TOOL` declares `description`, `prompt` and `run_in_background`, and is declared only on a Shell or background lane at child depth 0 while the `child_agent` kind gate is open. Admission refuses any other argument, `name` included, as belonging to the legacy Agent tool. A launch commits `child_run` through `HostedChildAgentSession.admit` under command id `childRunId = ${promptKey}:${callId}`, and a replay of the same call returns the committed record. Admission counts at most 4 active and 64 launched children per Session (`MANAGED_CHILD_LIMITS`). A background child answers the call at once; its result later arrives as a `<task-notification>` input (`childResultNotificationText`), and a failed or cancelled child sends no notification.
- **The relay and the cascade.** The Java `ChildResultRelay` creates, binds, watches and completes each `child_agent` run, then closes the child Session once its result is delivered: a child runs one turn and ends. `SessionLifecycleCoordinator.settle` cascades a parent close over every live `child_agent` run: stop request, the child Session's own close, then the `cancelled`/`stop_requested` terminal revision. A child of an open parent can be stopped by no other path; public child cancel is H4f (#13746).
- **The lifecycle gate.** Under a close or delete claim, the Java store admits `domain.committed` only for `hook_execution`, `hook_registration`, `child_run` and `child_acceptance`, and no input. Team records cannot commit while their lead Session closes.
- **Hosted tool plumbing.** A tool needs a declaration in `declarations()`, a `prepareRequests` branch, an execute branch, and exclusion from Broker acquire and Runtime reservation when it touches no Workspace (as `agent` does). Every domain a Hosted Session commits must be on the reopen whitelist in `verifyWorkspaceRestore`, or reopen fails with "Hosted recovery domain is unsupported." Approval asks for every tool outside the pre-approved lists in `default` and `auto-edit` modes, never in `yolo`; `HOSTED_INPUT_PREVIEW_TOOLS` must stay byte-identical to Java's `ManagedActionService.PREVIEW_TOOLS`.
- **Legacy.** A Config holds at most one team ("A team is already active. Delete it before creating a new one."), created by the leader alone. The Agent tool's `name` spawns a teammate into the active team and is silently ignored when no team exists; teammates always run concurrently, `run_in_background: false` is refused for them, and `plan_mode_required`, `read_only` and `model` have their own rules. Members are never removed from the roster, so a teammate that ended still holds its name and counts toward `MAX_TEAMMATES` (10). Teammates are long-lived: they report their final text to the leader on every idle transition and wait for the next message. `team_delete` force-aborts every teammate. `task_create` and `task_update` ask for approval by default; `task_update` validates self edges, missing tasks and cycles, mirrors each edge into both tasks, requires an owner for `in_progress`, and dispatches an assignment prompt to the owner. `task_list` lists the board and, for the leader, drains the leader's inbox.

## Decisions

1. **One open team per lead Session, run by the lead alone.** As in Legacy, a Session leads at most one open team (`active` or `closing`) at a time. The team tools are declared exactly where the Agent tool is (a Shell or background lane at child depth 0, with the `child_agent` kind gate open), and only while `team_state` and `team_task` are both enabled. A child Session never sees them: its depth is 1, so a member can neither create a team nor spawn members of its own. In H4e-b1 every team write is the lead's. Members gain board access with the mailbox (H4e-b2), because a member's write is a command from another Session to the lead's journal.
2. **`team_create` opens a team named in the Legacy form.** Its one parameter is `team_name`. The name is sanitized with the Legacy rule and must then match the H4e-a name form (at most 64 characters); an empty result is refused. The team's id is the call's key, `${promptKey}:${callId}`, so a replay of the call finds the committed team instead of opening a second one. The commit is the `team_state` opening: `active`, no members, `leadSessionId` this Session. Legacy's optional `description` is not carried, because the record has no field for it. A Managed team has no files and no process, so Legacy's global name directory and stale-PID reclaim have no counterpart: a team name is scoped to its lead Session.
3. **A member is a named, one-shot, background child agent.** The managed Agent tool admits `name` while a team is open. A launch with `name` is an H4b launch plus a roster join:
   - **Admission** adds team checks to H4b's own, which stay unchanged (closing parent, quotas, envelope). The team must be `active`; the sanitized name must be non-empty, not `leader`, and new to the roster; the roster must hold fewer than 10 members; `run_in_background` must not be `false`. `plan_mode_required`, `read_only`, `model` and `subagent_type` stay refused: plan approval is H4e-b3's, and a read-only or retyped member needs a definition this slice cannot apply (H4b decision 11). Within one tool batch, member names must be distinct, and a batch that spawns a member must not also create or delete the team or launch a foreground child, whose recovered wait (#13708) would answer the member as a plain background start.
   - **Execution** commits the H4b launch with completion `sent`, then the join: the next `team_state` revision, appending `{ name, childRunId, planModeRequired: false }`, under command id `${childRunId}:join`. Admission has checked every team rule the join enforces, and the lead's own writes are serial, so the join is refused in one case only: the run has already ended. H4e-a admits only a live run to the roster, and the relay works asynchronously, so a creation refusal can fail the run within seconds of its launch. The call then answers with the run's failure, the member never joins, and its name stays free.
   - **Without a team**, `name` is refused with a tool error instead of being ignored as in Legacy: an argument that silently means nothing is the dead switch H4b decision 11 refused.
   - **Lifetime.** A member is an H4b child: it runs one turn, its result is delivered, and the relay closes its Session. It cannot receive more work until members can be continued (H4e-b2). Its roster entry stays after it ends, as a Legacy member's does, so the cap of 10 counts members over the team's life, and H4b's cap of 4 active children bounds how many run at once, members and other children together.
   - **The crash window.** An executed call is not run again after a crash, so if the Session stops between the launch and the join, the launched child still runs and reports as an ordinary background child, never duplicated and never lost, and its name stays free (open question 1). Where the interrupted Turn's settlement answers the call (decision 11), it answers it as started, and as joined when the join committed. Only a re-driven batch, should one ever re-run the same call (same `childRunId`), replays the launch and completes the join while the run is still live. A run that ends before its join answers the call with that end: a failure as a failure, a completion as a finish whose result arrives unlabeled.
4. **Members report through H4b's notification, labeled.** A member's result reaches the lead the way every background child's does: an input committed with its acceptance, which wakes the lead. When the child run is on a roster, the notification carries a `<teammate>` element with the member's name. This is the counterpart of Legacy's automatic final report. A member that fails or is cancelled sends no notification, as for any H4b child; `task_list` shows its state (decision 6).
5. **The board is the lead's.** The three board tools follow their Legacy schemas and validations, with the differences listed in the Tools section:
   - **Identity.** A task's record id is `${teamId}#${number}`. Its `number` is one more than the highest number in the team, deleted tasks included, so a number is never reused. A number is allocated only for a call that has not yet committed its task (decision 11), so a replay answers with the number the call already took. The model names a task by its number, as in Legacy (`3` or `#3`).
   - **Content.** `subject` is inline text (at most 200 characters, as in Legacy). The description is stored as a Session resource named by `descriptionRef` (at most 10,000 characters in the tool, as in Legacy, within the record's 64 KiB). Metadata is a resource named by `metadataRef` (at most 32 KiB); `task_update` merges keys into it, and a `null` value deletes a key, as in Legacy.
   - **Dependencies.** `addBlockedBy` appends to the updated task's `blockedBy`; `addBlocks: [B]` appends the updated task to B's `blockedBy`, a revision of B. One call may therefore commit several records. Every edge is validated before the first commit (no self edge, every task present in the team, no cycle, no task past the record's 64 blockers), and the commits then run in order under command ids `${callKey}:${n}`. A replay skips the commits that already landed (decision 11), so it finishes a partly committed call and adds nothing twice. A task is blocked while any task in its `blockedBy` is neither `completed` nor `deleted`. Completing or deleting a blocker therefore needs no write to its dependents, where Legacy rewrites each one.
   - **Owners and status.** `in_progress` needs an owner, as in Legacy, and the lead has no implicit name, so it must name one. An owner that a call sets or changes is `leader` or a member whose run has not ended; `""` unassigns. A member whose run completed may also be set on a task the call leaves `completed`, so the board records who did the work: a member must join the roster before it can be named, and the physical pass showed that a one-shot member often completes before the lead's next call lands, which left such a member no way to own its task. A member that failed or was cancelled cannot be set. A task keeps the owner it has after that member's run ends, so the lead can complete, edit or delete the task without reassigning it. That is the usual flow, because a one-shot member has ended by the time its report arrives.
   - **No assignment delivery yet.** Without the mailbox, a member never learns of an assignment. `task_update` records the owner as a board fact, and its answer says the member was not notified. The lead conveys the work in the member's launch prompt.
   - **Deletion** is `status: "deleted"`, which ends the task's run and freezes it.
6. **`task_list` shows the board and the roster.** Its schema follows Legacy (`status`, `owner` and `blockedBy` filters), and each line is `#<number> [<status>] @<owner> — <subject>`, with the open blockers appended. Instead of Legacy's leader-inbox drain (there is no mailbox yet), the answer ends with the roster: each member with its run state (`running`, `completed`, `failed`, `cancelled`), read from its `child_run` record. This is how the lead learns that a member failed or that the team can be deleted.
7. **`team_delete` refuses while a member runs; otherwise it closes the team and deletes it.** Legacy force-aborts its teammates. On the Managed path the lead cannot stop a child of an open Session: the only stop path is its own Session's close cascade, and public child cancel is H4f's. Because members end on their own, `team_delete` refuses while any member's run has not ended, naming the running members. When none runs, it commits `closing` and then `deleted` under `${callKey}:closing` and `${callKey}:deleted`. A replay finishes a team left `closing` (decision 11), and a later `team_delete` finishes it too. Once H4f gives child cancel a route, `team_delete` can request a stop of running members instead of refusing (Follow-up work).
8. **The close cascade under teams is H4b's, and team records are not written during close (#13745 E3).** Members are `child_agent` runs, so closing the lead Session already does all three things E3 asks for. It cancels every running member through the H4b cascade (stop request, the member Session's own close, a terminal `cancelled`/`stop_requested` revision). Nothing interrupts a member's Turn in flight: the member Session's close is admitted only once that Turn ends on its own, so the lead stays `closing` until then. It never revives the lead for an orphaned result (H4b decisions 8 and 13). And it leaves each member's terminal fact separately observable as that member's own `child_run` revision. The team's own records stay as they were. A team's life is bounded by its lead Session's, and a reader treats a team whose lead Session is closing, closed or deleted as closed. This answers H4e-a open question 3. Committing `closing` and `deleted` under the claim would only restate the Session's status, and it would widen the lifecycle gate that today admits nothing but child and hook records. The Java lifecycle gate therefore does not change.
9. **Approval follows the Hosted rule, with one exception.** `team_create`, `team_delete`, `task_create` and `task_update` ask in `default` and `auto-edit` modes, like every Hosted tool outside the pre-approved lists, and like Legacy's board tools. `task_list` only reads the journal, so it joins the pre-approved lists of both modes, as `read_file` and `glob` do. A launch with `name` asks as any Agent launch does. `team_create`, `task_create` and `task_update` join `HOSTED_INPUT_PREVIEW_TOOLS` and Java's `PREVIEW_TOOLS` together, so an approval shows the team name, the subject, the owner and the status being approved.
10. **Enablement is per domain, after a physical pass.** Each team domain carries exactly one capability. So, unlike `child_run` (H4b decision 10), the plain enabled list is the right gate, and no per-capability gate is added. This answers the gate-shape question the #13745 triage raised. H4e-b1 landed its runtime (#13824) with `team_state` and `team_task` still off, the tests lifting the gate as the H4e-a suites do, and its enablement change adds both to `MANAGED_SESSION_ENABLED_DOMAINS` after a physical acceptance pass on a real Hosted stack, the ordering #13803 adopted after #13532. `team_message` and `team_plan` stay off. The reopen whitelist in `verifyWorkspaceRestore` admits `team_state` and `team_task`. Java needs only the preview list of decision 9: its store has validated the team bodies since H4e-a, so the server-first order holds.
11. **A replay resumes from what its call already committed.** Every team write commits under a command id derived from its call: `${callKey}` for `team_create` and `task_create`, `${childRunId}:join` for a join, `${callKey}:${n}` for the revisions of a `task_update`, and `${callKey}:closing` and `${callKey}:deleted` for `team_delete`. Before computing a write, the funnel looks its command id up with the authority's `committedExtensionOperation`. A command that has committed is done, and its committed record answers the call. Only a command that has not committed is computed from the current state. The lookup must come first, because a record rebuilt from the current state can differ from the one already committed: `task_create` would allocate the next number, and a later `task_update` revision would already include an earlier edge of the same call. The authority refuses a changed body under a committed command id as a conflict. This needs no record field: the command id is the call's own durable trace. A Turn that dies between a team write and its answer is not replayed. Every route that settles or resumes an interrupted Turn answers the journal-only calls it left before the model next reads the Turn: the channel settlement (whether or not the checkpoint bound the Turn), a user Turn's cancellation takeover, the cancel and continue routes of a parked Turn, the bare load of a Hooks or publication Session (including a Hooks Session's file-history gate), and a crashed wake Turn's aftermath. A team call whose command committed is answered as committed in full or in part, pointing the model to `task_list`, and a background launch as started (and joined, when its join committed), never as a call that never ran: core's orphan repair would otherwise tell the next model to retry the call, and the retry would open a second task or member. A route that resumes the Turn also answers a team or agent call that committed nothing as never run, because the resume needs its round whole (a pending file history's check refuses it otherwise) and no Runtime settlement answers such a call. A route that only settles the Turn leaves such a call with the answer the route already gave.

## A member's life

| Step | Commit                                                         | By                                            |
| ---- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | `child_run` launch (`sent`)                                    | the Agent call (H4b)                          |
| 2    | `team_state` join (`membershipRevision + 1`)                   | the same Agent call                           |
| 3–6  | dispatch, attach, settle, accept with the labeled notification | the H4b relay, unchanged except for the label |
| 7    | consumption                                                    | the lead's wake turn (H4b)                    |

A lead close at any step after 1 runs the H4b cascade over the member's run (decision 8). `team_delete` waits for step 4 or a failure end of every member (decision 7).

- **Everything H4e-b2 and H4e-b3 own**: the mailbox, `send_message` to a member, assignment delivery, member-side board tools, continued members, plan approval and `plan_mode_required`, `request_shutdown`, legacy team import, and the enablement of `team_message` and `team_plan`.
- **Stopping a running member** before it ends (H4f gives child cancel its route) and **detach** (its own follow-up).
- **Read-only, retyped or differently modeled members**, which need definition fields applied to execution (D8b/D8c).
- **Any public contract change.** No route, OpenAPI or Flyway change; a public team resource belongs with #13785.

## Validation plan

- **Tool turn.** The admission matrix for every tool and every refusal of decisions 2, 3, 5 and 7, including the batch rules. Replay of each tool call: a replayed call adds no team, member, task or edge. A crash after `task_create` commits its task and before the tool result: the replay answers with the same number and opens no second task. The crash between launch and join, in both outcomes. Task numbering after deletions. Completing, editing and deleting a task whose owner's run has ended. Multi-record edge updates that stop part-way and then replay. The roster view in `task_list`. The `<teammate>` label. Approval classes and preview.
- **Authority.** No new rule, so the H4e-a suites stand. A rebuild of a Session holding team records restores them, and the reopen whitelist admits them.
- **Java.** A lead close with an open team and running members runs the H4b cascade over the members and commits no team record, and the lifecycle gate still refuses team records under a claim.
- **Physical acceptance**, before enablement, on a real Hosted stack:
  - a lead creates a team, spawns two members by name, assigns board tasks, receives both labeled reports, marks the tasks done and deletes the team;
  - a lead closes with a member still running, and the member is cancelled while the team records stay unchanged;
  - the Harness is killed between a member's launch and its join: the child runs on as an ordinary background child, there is exactly one `child_run`, and no second roster entry ever appears.
- **Physical acceptance results** (2026-10-10, aarch64 Linux host; packaged Spring server with its Store, Broker, relay and coordinator, the packaged Hosted Harness, durable local workers, MariaDB, and a real model, `qwen3.8-max`, in `default` approval mode; the plan and its outcomes are in `.qwen/e2e-tests/h4e-b1-team-lead-runtime.md`):
  - The full team flow is observed. The `team_state` chain is the opening, two joins, `closing` and `deleted`. Both reports reach the lead with their `<teammate>` labels, consumed inside the running Turn in one run and as wake Turns in another. The first run found that both members completed before the lead's `task_update` assigned them, so the board refused them as owners. Decision 5's finished-owner rule answers that, and the re-run at the enablement head records each member as the owner of its completed task.
  - A lead close with a member still running cancels the member: its `child_run` ends `cancelled` with its stop request, its Session closes, the team records are unchanged and the lead is never revived. The close waits for the member's Turn to end (decision 8), and that includes an approval the member is waiting on.
  - Killing the Harness before the join commits leaves one `child_run` and no roster entry; killing it after the join commits leaves one `child_run` and one roster entry. In both arms the member Session runs, but the lead's Turn fails `managed_runtime_recovery_blocked`, so the member's result never reaches the lead (open question 5). The same run without a team, with an unnamed background launch, ends the same way. The channel settlement's answer (decision 11) is not exercised, because the rig has no channel adapter.
  - `team_delete` with a member running is refused, naming the member, and commits no revision. `task_list` runs without an approval card, and the `team_create`, `task_create`, `task_update` and Agent cards show their input previews. A control arm that removes only the two domains from the enabled list declares no team tool and no `name`, refuses `name` with the unsupported-argument answer, and commits no record.
  - The pass needs one declared rig step: a fresh Session is moved to `hosted-workspace-shell/1` by SQL before its first Turn. No Java route creates a Shell-laned Session today: a public Workspace Session gets `hosted-workspace-files/1`, the public create refuses a profile override, and a child Session copies its parent's profile. So, like H4b's Agent tool, the team tools reach users only once a public route opens a Shell profile.

## Acceptance criteria

- With `team_state` and `team_task` enabled, a Hosted lead can create a team, spawn named members, keep a board and delete the team, and each member's result reaches the lead labeled with its name.
- Every admission refusal in decisions 2, 3, 5 and 7 answers as a tool error and commits nothing.
- A replayed call never opens a second team, member, task or edge, a replayed `task_create` answers with the number it already took, and a call stopped part-way finishes on replay.
- Closing the lead cancels its running members through the H4b cascade, never revives the lead for an orphaned result, leaves each member's terminal fact observable, and writes no team record.
- While either domain is disabled, no team tool is declared and `name` stays refused. Enablement flips only after the physical pass, and the tests that pin the disabled state flip deliberately.

## Open questions

1. **Reconciling a member left off the roster.** A crash between launch and join that is never replayed leaves a running child that no roster names. An Agent call writes no `tool.intent`, but its arguments, `name` included, stay in the journaled assistant message, so a reconciler could finish the join after recovery. This slice accepts the degraded outcome (an ordinary background child) and leaves the reconciliation open. On a Turn that is not a channel Turn, the crash also blocks the lead itself (open question 5), which a reconciler would need first.
2. **Members' share of the active cap.** Members and other children share H4b's 4 active children per Session, where Legacy runs up to 10 teammates at once. Whether a team should get its own active bound is open until real teams show the need.
3. **Making a member useful twice.** A one-shot member cannot take more work. Continuing a member (H4e-a open question 4) is H4e-b2's, together with the mailbox that would carry the new work.
4. **A Hooks Session interrupted outside a Runtime park.** Every recovery route now answers journal-only calls (decision 11). A Hooks Session never takes over, though, and its bare load resumes only a Turn parked at `results_ready`. A Turn interrupted inside a batch with no Runtime work (team calls only) is answered by the load but stays recovery-blocked, as any Hooks Session interrupted mid-model-round already does. Team calls widen that window. Recovering such Turns belongs with the Hooks runtime, and the enabling step should settle it before teams run on Hooks Sessions.
5. **A crash inside a background launch blocks the lead.** A Harness that dies after a launch commits and before the Turn's next checkpoint leaves the Turn parked at `model_output_committed`, a model-start phase that a replacement Harness declines to take over, so the coordinator fails the Turn `managed_runtime_recovery_blocked`. The lead's journal still holds that parked Turn, so every later load of the lead answers `hosted_turn_recovery_required`: the relay cannot record the running child's dispatch, attach or result, and every later Turn fails the same way. The physical pass hit this on a Shell Session with and without a team, so it is a limit of H4b's recovery, not of teams; open question 4 is the same window on a Hooks Session. Team calls widen it, as they do there, and the enablement accepts it as H4b's background launches already do, because declaring no team tools would not close it for those launches.
